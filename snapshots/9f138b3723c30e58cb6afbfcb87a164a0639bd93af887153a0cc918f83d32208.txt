II. DISCOVER
Y
A. Motions
to Dismiss and Staying Discovery
The fil
ing of a motion to dismiss does not stay discovery. Rather, the parties are required
to comply with the rules of discovery as required by the Local Rules and the Federal Rules of Civil
Procedure. See LR 26-1 et. seq.; Fed. R. Civ. Pro. 26 et. seq . If a party believes that discovery
should be stayed pending a decision on the motion to dismiss, the party is required to file a motion
to stay. Judge Couvillier follows the pragmatic approach and all briefs regarding staying discovery
pending ruling on a motion to dismiss must address the factors articulated in Schrader v. Wynn Las
Vegas, LLC, No. 2:19-cv-02159-JCM-BNW, 2021 WL 4810324, at *4 (D. Nev. Oct. 14, 2021); see
also Aristocrat Techs., Inc. v. Light & Wonder, Inc ., No. 2:24- cv-00382-GMN-MDC, 2024 WL
2302151, at *3 (D. Nev. May 21, 2024).
B. Discov
ery Disputes
Pursuant
to LR 1- 1(b)(2), Judge Couvillier requires the parties to follow the procedure s
outlined below regarding any discovery dispute. No discovery motion may be filed until the
following procedure has been followed and such motion is ordered by Judge Couvillier:
1. If a dis
covery dispute arises the parties must first meet and confer to try to resolve
their dispute in compliance with LR IA 1-3(f). Lead counsel for each party shall meet and confer
2
in person, videoconference, or telephone. A mere exchange of letters or e -mails does not satisfy
the meet and confer requirement.
2. If the parties are unable to resolve their dispute informally after a good-faith effort,
the parties must file a single document captioned “Stipulation Regarding Discovery Dispute” that
addresses each one of the following items:
i. A brief, joint summary of the discovery dispute not to exceed one (1) page;
ii. A restatement of the full text of the discovery requests, or deposition question, in
dispute as originally stated and the responses to the requests. This may be done in single-spaced,
10-font. A joint summary of the meet-and-confer efforts as required by LR IA 1-3(f), which
includes: (1) a summary of discussions; (2) identification of time, place, manner, and
participants; and (3) certification from counsel that, despite a sincere effort, the parties were
unable to resolve or narrow the dispute without court intervention. This joint meet-and-confer
summary may not exceed two (2) pages.
iii. An explanation, without boilerplate language, of the position taken by each side,
not to exceed two (2) pages per side. Such explanation must include relevant authorities
supporting the parties’ respective position. It is not necessary to include case law of general
discovery principles. Rather, the parties should include case law addressing their specific
position or matters at issue.
iv. Each side must make a proposal of compromise or resolution of the disputed
discovery (e.g., request, interrogatory, deposition question). The proposal may not exceed two
(2) pages per side.
3. Upon review of the filed “Stipulation Regarding Discovery Dispute,” Judge
Couvillier may set a telephonic conference, order written briefing, or enter an order deciding the
dispute without conference or briefing.
C. Emergency Discovery Disputes
In emergencies during discovery events – which should be exceedingly rare - such as
depositions, any party may, after exhausting good faith attempts to resolve disputed issues, seek
judicial intervention pursuant to LR 26-6(d) by contacting Judge Couvillier through his law clerks.
Before calling Judge Couvillier’s chambers, the parties must first send a short email
describing the emergency and nature of the dispute to court mdc@nvd.uscourts.gov . After
sending such email, the parties may call chambers at 702-464-5540.
If Judge Couvillier is unavailable, the deposition or other discovery event shall proceed
with objections noted for the record and resolved later via the “Stipulation Regarding Discovery
Dispute” process addressed above.

III. ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE (AI)
Any counsel or party may use AI to draft documents or process discovery consistent with
Rule 11 of the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure (Fed. R. Civ. P.), this Standing Order, and any
other applicable legal or ethical obligations.
1. Certification of AI Use In Drafting Filings. Any party that files (manually
or e-filing) a brief, motion, request, pleading, or other similar document with the Court which
includes content generated or drafted by artificial intelligence (i.e., ChatGPT, Harvey, CoCounsel,
Google Gemini, or any other such application) must include the following certification above the
signature line:
“I certify that artificial intelligence was used to generate a portion of
this filing. I have reviewed the source material and verified that the
artificially generated content in the filing is accurate and complies
with the filer’s Rule 11 obligations.”
Failure to include such certification may result in sanctions.
2. Citations to Authorities. A pro se party or a counsel’s failure to confirm
or double-check the accuracy, veracity, or existence of a case or legal citation (or assertion of fact)
obtained from an AI tool or created by an AI tool is grounds for potential sanctions. All citations
to authority or assertions of fact must be corroborated and comply with Fed. R. Civ. P. 11 and LR
IA 7-3.
3. Protective Orders. Parties are strongly encouraged to address the use of
AI in proposed protective orders. In particular, such orders should include protocols addressing
the type of permissible AI systems (e.g., closed AI system v. open AI system) and any limitations
relative to using AI tools for processing discovery materials that contain personal-data identifiers,
confidential material, trade secrets, or similar.
4. Sealed Documents and Court Documents Containing Personal -Data
Identifiers May Not Be Uploaded to Open AI System. Counsel and parties are prohibited from
uploading onto any open AI system - where the source code, learning materials, and underling
models may be publicly accessible - any filed documents which: (a) have been filed under seal; or
(b) contain personal-data identifiers listed in LR IC 6-1(a)(1)-(6). Violation may result in sanctions.
IV. OPPORTUNITIES FOR LESS-EXPERIENCED ATTORNEYS
Per LR 78-1, all motions may be considered and decided with or without a hearing. Any
party making or opposing a motion who believes oral argument may assist the court may request
a hearing by inserting the words ORAL ARGUMENT REQUESTED below the title of the
document on the first page of the motion or response.
Judge Couvillier encourages litigants to permit more junior members of the litigation team
to present oral argument and gain courtroom experience. Thus, if a party requests oral argument,
the party should further note in the caption of the motion that a junior, less experienced attorney
will be responsible for presenting argument. Allowing junior, less experienced attorneys to argue
will weigh in favor of holding a hearing.

VI. REQUESTS FOR COURT ACTION
When a party requests the Court to act on a filing, the party s hould caption and file-code
such filing as a “Stipulation” ; “Motion”; (opposed, joint , or unopposed) ; or “Application”
(opposed, joint, or unopposed). That will flag such filing and bring it to the Court’s attention.
Documents captioned and filed as “Notices”; “Reports”; “Status Reports”; and the like may not be
flagged for the Judge’s review and action.

Counsel and parties should not contact Judge Couvillier’s chambers ex parte - except as

Couvillier’s Courtroom Deputy, Tawnee Renfro, at (702) 464-5427, with any scheduling or other
non-substantive issues. See LR IA 7-2(c). Substantive matters should be addressed through filings
that are served upon all parties.
